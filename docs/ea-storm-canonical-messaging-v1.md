## 1. Brand

**EA STORM**

EA STORM is the master external brand. Distinguish the full brand EA STORM from the execution capability Storm. At the brand level, EA STORM describes the complete product promise; within the architecture, Crystal Ball and Storm have distinct roles.

## 2. Brand Promise

**Keep What Matters Moving.**

This is the durable external promise. Do not replace it with Decision & Execution Intelligence, organizational intelligence, judgment intelligence, reasoning infrastructure, distribution intelligence, agent orchestration, or AI operating system. Those concepts may support deeper technical or investor explanation; they are not the master customer-facing promise.

## 3. Core Problem

**Important things get lost between knowing and doing.**

Information can exist across systems. Context can exist across people. Ownership can cross teams. Execution can happen somewhere else.

The organization may possess the information without turning it into the right action at the right time.

Do not lead with fragmented systems, data silos, decision intelligence, organizational judgment, or workflow orchestration. These may explain causes or mechanics later. The customer-facing problem remains: Important things get lost between knowing and doing.

## 4. One-Liner

**EA STORM identifies what needs attention, gets it to the right owner, and keeps it moving through the outcome.**

Preserve this wording as the primary short explanation. Do not make it more technical or add AI-powered, agentic, intelligence layer, decision intelligence, judgment layer, or orchestration platform.

## 5. Three Customer Questions

- What matters?
- Who owns it?
- What happens next?

These are recurring operating questions, not a new category name.

## 6. Customer Explanation

**EA STORM works across the systems you already use to identify what needs attention, clarify ownership and next action, and follow the work through the result.**

Use this when more explanation is required. EA STORM works across the existing environment; it is not positioned as another replacement CRM, dashboard, or system of record.

## 7. Differentiation

**Most systems help you record work, report on work, or execute work. EA STORM makes sure the right work moves in the first place.**

Supporting statement:

**It works across the systems you already use instead of becoming another system your team has to manage.**

Position EA STORM as complementary to the existing stack. Do not attack CRM, ERP, BI, workflow, AI-agent, or collaboration platforms.

## 8. Crystal Ball

**Crystal Ball answers the questions that have to be right before work moves.**

Crystal Ball questions include:

- What matters?
- Why now?
- Who owns it?
- What should happen next?
- What authority is required?
- What oversight is needed?
- Should a human do it?
- Can AI help?
- How will we know it happened?
- What was the outcome?
- What did we learn?
- What problem did we solve?

Crystal Ball is not prediction or fortune telling. It is the reasoning/triage discipline that protects the questions that cannot be dropped. Do not lead public/customer language with "judgment layer" unless technical context requires it.

**Crystal Ball determines what requires attention/action. Crystal Ball does not execute the work.**

## 9. Storm

**Storm keeps the right work moving once the answers are clear.**

Supporting explanation:

**It carries authorized work through the right person, AI, agent, workflow or system—and follows it through the result.**

**Storm = execution / orchestration.**

Storm does not determine what matters, perform Crystal Ball triage, replace executive judgment, establish decision rights by itself, or decide what deserves attention. Crystal Ball and Storm must remain distinct.

## 10. Product Role Architecture

Crystal Ball determines.

Authority governs.

Storm executes.

Verification proves.

Memory learns.

This is shorthand for deeper technical/product explanation. Do not force the entire phrase into every customer-facing surface. Authority governs what may proceed before Storm executes authorized work.

## 11. Why Now — Customer

**Your systems know more than your organization acts on.**

More data, more software and more AI create more possible actions. EA STORM helps determine which ones actually need to move.

Keep this simple.

## 12. Why Now — Investor

**AI can do more work than ever. The harder question is what work should move.**

Do not make Judgment is scarce, Decision intelligence, Distribution intelligence, or Reasoning infrastructure the primary market thesis. Avoid competing in the AI jargon/category race.

## 13. Buyer Trigger Territory

Approved campaign/discovery trigger lines:

- “How did we miss this?”
- “I thought someone owned that.”
- “Why am I just hearing about this?”
- “We had the information. Why didn’t we act on it?”
- “Why am I still holding all of this together?”

Use these for campaign concepts, LinkedIn content, deck openings, discovery conversations, advertising, and customer pain sections. They are not all taglines and must not replace Keep What Matters Moving.

## 14. Origin Story

Canonical hook:

**Some things can bounce.**

**The important things can’t drop.**

### Supplied facts

Early in Kristina Spencer’s career, in one of her first meetings with a commander, the commander had two lucite boxes. One contained a crystal ball. One contained a tennis/bouncy ball.

The commander used the Crystal Ball to represent the questions that could not be dropped:

- Why does this matter?
- Who should own it?
- Should it be automated?
- What oversight is required?
- How will it be held accountable?
- What was the outcome?
- What did we learn?
- What problem did we solve?

The Crystal Ball questions cannot drop because the ball would shatter.

Execution was represented by the bouncy/tennis ball. Execution can be handed to the team. It can move. It may occasionally drop. But it can be recovered because the ball bounces.

Years later, Kristina was discussing an operational frustration with co-founder Monica Vasquez. Monica told Kristina to throw the ball to Monica’s service animal, Storm.

Kristina watched Storm catch the ball, move with it, and bring it back. Sometimes the ball fell. Storm picked it back up and returned.

That moment connected the earlier operating lesson to the system that eventually became Crystal Ball and Storm.

### Canonical interpretation

That became the operating idea behind EA STORM: answer the questions that matter, move the right work, and bring the result back.

Do not embellish the story. Do not invent names, dates, military details, or quotations not supplied. Do not disclose medical information about Monica. Do not turn Storm into a mascot. Preserve the supplied tennis/bouncy-ball wording rather than inventing specificity.
